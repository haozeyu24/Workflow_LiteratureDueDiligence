# Topic

Protein-level regulation of PAX transcription factors, with emphasis on PAX8 stability and targetability in oncology.

The run should retrieve and review literature that can explain how PAX8 or related PAX proteins are folded, stabilized, destabilized, degraded, imported into the nucleus, retained in the nucleus, or accumulated in specific cellular compartments. The review should be especially sensitive to protein-protein interactions that may modulate PAX protein behavior, including partner transcription factors, cofactors, chromatin remodelers, chaperones, ubiquitin/proteasome regulators, and nuclear transport proteins.

The main biological question is not what genes PAX8 regulates. It is what molecular mechanisms act on PAX8 or analogous PAX proteins as proteins, and which of those mechanisms could be disrupted to reduce oncogenic PAX8 protein stability or nuclear function.

## Retrieval Scope Notes

- PubMed query drivers: PAX8/PAX/PAX family terms combined with protein stability, degradation, ubiquitination, proteasome, half-life, turnover, nuclear localization, nuclear import, nuclear export, nuclear retention, protein interaction, cofactor, chromatin remodeler, chaperone, post-translational modification, phosphorylation, SUMOylation, acetylation, structural domain, folding, conformation, and localization motifs.
- Comparator scope: Other PAX family members may be queried directly when paired with the same protein-level mechanism classes, because shared paired-domain/homeodomain architecture and related transcription factor biology may reveal mechanisms relevant to PAX8.
- Secondary synthesis context: Cancer relevance, lineage dependency, oncogenic transcription factor targeting, ovarian/renal/thyroid cancer PAX8 biology, and PAX family disease biology may inform interpretation but should not dominate first-pass search terms unless tied to protein-level mechanism evidence.
- Deferred adjacent biology: PAX transcriptional targets, gene-expression regulation, enhancer binding, developmental differentiation programs, and clinical biomarker correlations without protein-level mechanistic information.

Potential evidence signals include cycloheximide chase, proteasome inhibition, ubiquitination assays, co-immunoprecipitation, proximity labeling, mutational mapping, nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation, immunofluorescence localization, nuclear transport motif mapping, structural studies, domain-deletion experiments, and perturbation of interaction partners that changes PAX protein abundance or nuclear localization.
