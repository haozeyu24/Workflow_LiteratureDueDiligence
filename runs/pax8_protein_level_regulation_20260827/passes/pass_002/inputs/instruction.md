# Instruction

Support a review article on mechanisms that regulate PAX transcription factor proteins at the protein level, with PAX8 as the main oncology-relevant focus and other PAX family members as authorized comparators where they may reveal shared or analogous regulatory mechanisms.

Prioritize papers that provide direct or mechanistically useful evidence about:

- PAX8 protein folding, conformational stability, structural domains, disorder, or mutation effects on protein stability/localization.
- PAX8 protein abundance, half-life, degradation, ubiquitination, proteasome or lysosome involvement, or degron-like mechanisms.
- PAX8 nuclear import, nuclear export, nuclear retention, subcellular localization, nuclear accumulation, or sequence motifs controlling localization.
- Protein-protein interactions involving PAX8 and partner transcription factors, cofactors, chromatin remodelers, chaperones, E3 ligases, deubiquitinases, SUMO/ubiquitin pathway components, kinases, phosphatases, importins/exportins, or related regulators when the interaction affects or plausibly controls PAX protein stability, retention, localization, folding, or degradation.
- Similar protein-level mechanisms for other PAX family members, including PAX1, PAX2, PAX3, PAX4, PAX5, PAX6, PAX7, and PAX9, when the mechanism could inform hypotheses for PAX8 targeting.

Deprioritize or exclude papers that are only about transcriptional control of PAX gene expression, PAX DNA-binding sites, downstream target genes, lineage specification, developmental phenotypes, biomarkers, or oncogenic transcriptional programs unless they include direct evidence about the PAX protein itself or a PAX-containing protein complex relevant to protein stability, localization, retention, folding, or degradation.

Useful final reading-set papers should enable a mechanistic map of potentially targetable PAX protein regulation: domains and motifs, post-translational modifications, interaction partners, nuclear trafficking/retention mechanisms, degradation machinery, and experimental assays that could be adapted to test PAX8 protein destabilization in cancer models.

## Query Scope Contract

- primary entities: PAX8 is primary. Authorized PAX-family comparators are PAX1, PAX2, PAX3, PAX4, PAX5, PAX6, PAX7, PAX9, and generic PAX/Paired box transcription factor literature when protein-level mechanisms are discussed.
- declared mechanism classes for PubMed retrieval: protein folding/conformation/structure-stability relationships; protein stability/abundance/half-life; degradation/proteolysis/ubiquitination/proteasome/lysosome; post-translational modification that affects protein stability or localization; nuclear import/export/localization/retention/accumulation; protein-protein interaction with cofactors, partner transcription factors, chromatin remodelers, chaperones, ubiquitin-system regulators, and nuclear transport machinery when connected to PAX protein behavior.
- authorized comparator entities or systems: other PAX family proteins across mammalian, cancer, developmental, and biochemical systems; disease contexts including ovarian, renal, thyroid, B-cell, sarcoma, neurodevelopmental, and developmental biology models are allowed only as contexts for protein-level PAX mechanism evidence.
- secondary context for synthesis only: oncology targeting rationale for PAX8; PAX-dependent cancer cell survival; lineage addiction; transcriptional output and downstream genes; chromatin occupancy and enhancer biology.
- adjacent biology deferred from first-pass retrieval: general PAX transcriptional regulatory networks, PAX gene expression regulation, broad developmental phenotypes, biomarker-only clinical studies, GWAS-only associations, and target-gene catalog papers without direct PAX protein-level evidence.

Reviewer rule: at abstract screening, include borderline papers if they mention PAX protein interactions, localization, ubiquitination, post-translational modification, structural domains, mutation effects, or nuclear trafficking even when the abstract does not explicitly state a stability endpoint.

## Learned Guidance From PMC Feedback

Pass-1 PMC full-text review confirmed useful in-scope mechanism families around PAX protein degradation/stability, ubiquitin/proteasome regulation, SUMO/PTM biology, nuclear localization/retention, PAX-containing protein complexes, and structural/domain mechanisms. For pass 2, retain those families but tighten broad comparator retrieval:

- Prefer queries where PAX terms co-occur with explicit degradation/stability, PTM, nuclear-localization, PPI, domain, or disorder language.
- Avoid generic `protein` and generic `nuclear` anchors unless they are paired with localization, import, export, retention, degradation, PTM, or interaction terms.
- Treat PAX expression, lineage markers, downstream transcriptional programs, and general development/cancer phenotypes as exclusion signals unless paired with PAX-near protein-level evidence.
- Add rescue sensitivity for protein complex/interactome, co-immunoprecipitation, paired-domain/homeodomain, and intrinsically disordered region language.
