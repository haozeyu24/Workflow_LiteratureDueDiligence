# Instruction

This run supports a review-style question about how PAX-family transcription
factors are controlled at the protein level. Retrieval should be family-wide,
not PAX8-only, while later synthesis should highlight what seems most relevant
to destabilizing or mislocalizing PAX8 in oncology.

Prioritize papers with direct or plausibly direct evidence that a PAX-family
protein undergoes one or more of the following:

1. protein stabilization or destabilization
2. ubiquitination, deubiquitination, proteasomal degradation, lysosomal or
   autophagic turnover
3. phosphorylation, acetylation, SUMOylation, methylation, UFMylation, or
   other post-translational modification tied to abundance, localization, or
   turnover
4. folding or chaperone dependence that affects PAX protein abundance or
   conformational state
5. nuclear import, export, retention, sequestration, or accumulation
6. interaction-dependent regulation through partner transcription factors,
   cofactors, E3 ligases, deubiquitinases, chromatin remodelers, or other
   protein complexes

Abstract inclusion should be relatively recall-friendly for this run class.
Comparator PAX-family mechanism papers should survive abstract review when they
mention a named PAX family factor plus a plausible protein-level mechanism plus
an outcome such as stability, degradation, localization, retention, turnover,
or steady-state protein level.

Deprioritize or exclude papers focused only on:

- transcriptional regulation of PAX gene expression
- downstream genes regulated by PAX factors without protein-level regulation of
  the PAX factor itself
- diagnostic-marker or immunohistochemistry usage without mechanistic protein
  evidence
- recombinase-driver nomenclature such as `Pax8-Cre` or `Pax7-CreER` when the
  PAX factor is not itself the mechanistic subject
- broad developmental or lineage-expression studies without direct protein-level
  mechanism

A useful final reading set should contain:

- direct mechanistic papers on any PAX family member with clear evidence on
  stability, degradation, PTM, localization, or partner-dependent regulation
- especially informative papers on PAX8, PAX2, PAX3, PAX5, and PAX7
- a minority of review or synthesis papers that help position a future PAX8
  oncology-focused interpretation

## Query Scope Contract

- primary entities:
  `PAX family`, `PAX8`, `PAX2`, `PAX3`, `PAX5`, and `PAX7`
- declared mechanism classes for PubMed retrieval:
  protein stability, protein degradation, ubiquitination, deubiquitination,
  post-translational modification, folding/chaperone control, nuclear
  localization/import/export/retention/accumulation, and interaction-dependent
  stabilization or relocalization
- declared outcomes or required evidence claims:
  stability, steady-state protein level, half-life, degradation, turnover,
  ubiquitination, SUMOylation, phosphorylation, localization, nuclear
  retention, nuclear accumulation, import/export, or partner-dependent control
- authorized comparator entities or systems:
  all PAX family members are first-class evidence when the mechanism is about
  PAX protein regulation
- evidence insufficient by itself:
  transcriptional regulation, target-gene regulation, marker studies,
  recombinase-driver references, and non-mechanistic association papers
- secondary context for synthesis only:
  oncology motivation, lineage-survival framing, and the question of whether a
  family-shared mechanism could be exploited against PAX8
- adjacent biology deferred from first-pass retrieval:
  downstream transcriptional programs, general developmental atlases, and
  PAX-adjacent chromatin biology without PAX protein handling

Pass-2 learned guidance:

- preserve family-wide comparator retrieval because pass 1 showed that useful
  mechanistic signal is distributed across multiple PAX paralogs
- explicitly rescue terms around ubiquitination, protein degradation,
  post-translational modification, deubiquitination, protein stability, nuclear
  localization, nuclear retention, and PPI-dependent control
- keep `PAX8` highlighted in retrieval, but do not require it for inclusion
- demote marker/immunohistochemistry papers, recombinase-driver nomenclature,
  and target-gene/transcription papers unless they also show direct PAX
  protein-level evidence
