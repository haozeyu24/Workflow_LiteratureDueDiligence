# Review Frame

- parent field:
  post-translational regulation of transcription factors and oncogenic
  dependency on lineage-associated TFs
- introduction background scope:
  why PAX-family transcription factors matter biologically and why protein-level
  targeting strategies are attractive when direct inhibition is difficult
- bigger-field progress to summarize:
  known mechanisms for destabilizing or relocalizing transcription factors and
  whether PAX-family proteins show analogous vulnerabilities
- foundational concepts or older terminology to preserve:
  paired-box transcription factors, PAX developmental regulators, and classic
  PAX partner/cofactor studies when they contain mechanistic protein-level clues
- review-architecture paper types worth retaining:
  direct mechanistic studies, family-comparator studies, and a small number of
  framing reviews
- perspective questions:
  which mechanisms appear family-shared versus paralog-specific; which seem most
  plausible to exploit for PAX8 destabilization in cancer
- controversies or unresolved gaps:
  sparse direct PAX8 protein-regulation literature, uncertain transferability
  across family members, and frequent confusion between transcriptional and
  post-translational regulation
- translational or practical outlook:
  candidate intervention points that might reduce PAX8 stability or nuclear
  retention

## Retention guidance

Papers may be retained partly because they serve one of these review roles:

- `direct_mechanistic`
- `clinical_translational`
- `foundational_background`
- `field_synthesis`
- `perspective_gap`

Use these roles to justify a minority of context-setting papers without letting
them overtake the direct-mechanism corpus.
