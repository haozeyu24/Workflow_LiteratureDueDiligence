# Review Frame

- parent field: PI3K-pathway targeted therapy and precision oncology in cancer, with emphasis on PI3K-alpha-selective inhibition.
- introduction background scope: PIK3CA/PI3K-alpha biology, oncogenic PI3K pathway activation, approved and investigational PI3K-alpha inhibitors, tumor-type context, and why resistance limits durable benefit.
- bigger-field progress to summarize: movement from pan-PI3K or less selective agents toward alpha-selective and mutant-selective inhibitors; trial development of alpelisib, inavolisib, STX-478/STX478, RLY-2608/RLY2608, and related agents; emergence of rational combinations.
- foundational concepts or older terminology to preserve: PIK3CA-mutant cancer; p110-alpha; PI3K alpha; PI3Kalpha; PI3K-alpha selective inhibitor; endocrine resistance; adaptive feedback; bypass signaling; pathway reactivation; acquired resistance; intrinsic resistance.
- review-architecture paper types worth retaining: direct mechanistic resistance studies, clinical trial primary reports, subgroup/biomarker reports, translational biopsy or ctDNA analyses, combination-rationale studies, prior reviews that map the field, and negative or failed trial reports when they clarify resistance or therapeutic limits.
- perspective questions: Which resistance mechanisms are best supported by direct evidence? Which are inferred from pathway biology or combination rationale? Which mechanisms arise from lab models versus patient evidence? Which inhibitor-specific versus class-wide patterns are visible? Which combinations have been tested to overcome resistance, and did they succeed?
- controversies or unresolved gaps: whether laboratory resistance models predict patient progression; whether combination trials overcome resistance or mainly improve early response; how toxicity and dose intensity affect combination feasibility; whether newer alpha- or mutant-selective inhibitors bypass old resistance mechanisms or create new ones.
- translational or practical outlook: organize evidence so a reader can see actionable resistance hypotheses, trial-backed strategies, failed approaches, and remaining access gaps for non-PMC full text.

## Retention guidance

Papers may be retained partly because they serve one of these review roles:

- `direct_mechanistic`
- `clinical_translational`
- `foundational_background`
- `field_synthesis`
- `perspective_gap`

Use these roles to justify a minority of context-setting papers without letting
them overtake the direct-mechanism corpus.
